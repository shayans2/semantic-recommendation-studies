\begin{abstract}
We reproduce selected released-code behavior and study the user loss in
RLMRec-Con with a LightGCN backbone on
Amazon-book, Yelp and Steam. Removing it changes mean test Recall@20 by
$-0.00027$, $-0.00015$ and $-0.00139$ over five paired seeds. Both full and
item-only objectives outperform the collaborative baseline; only Steam
provides paired $t$-test evidence of a user-term benefit after multiplicity
adjustment. The other intervals do not establish equivalence. A diagnostic
explains a much larger apparent effect on Yelp: longer patience lets the full
model recover from about $0.038$ to $0.116$ validation Recall@20. Fixed-seed
repeats also vary on the scale of the small Amazon mean contrast. This
objective-level ablation identifies a conditional user-term benefit and shows
how premature stopping can exaggerate an apparent benefit from its removal.

\keywords{Recommendation \and Language models \and Reproducibility \and Ablation studies}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RLMRec aligns collaborative representations with generated
user and item profiles~\cite{rlmrec}. An improvement from this combined
objective does not establish how much each alignment term contributes.
Separating their effects matters when choosing whether a recommender needs a
user-profile generation pipeline in addition to item semantics.

The original RLMRec ablation (Section 4.3) examines shuffled semantic
representations and alternative text encoders~\cite{rlmrec}. It does not
report our intervention: deleting the user-alignment loss while keeping the
item losses and released embeddings fixed. Corrupting semantic targets and
removing one loss answer different questions. The former tests the relevance
of semantic correspondence; the latter tests a term's marginal contribution.

We test this intervention on three benchmarks and diagnose a high-weight
Yelp failure using longer patience. Fixed-seed repeats check numerical
repeatability. The contribution is an objective ablation that separates a
conditional user-term benefit from a stopping artifact. This retrospective
study does not propose a new state-of-the-art model.

\section{Context and Compared Objectives}
LightGCN propagates ID embeddings over the interaction graph~\cite{lightgcn}.
RLMRec adds alignment to generated profiles~\cite{rlmrec}. AlphaRec already
shows that item language representations can support recommendation and
examines alternative RLMRec inputs~\cite{alpharec}; our intervention instead
keeps the released inputs fixed and deletes one alignment term. An earlier
FREEDOM-inspired graph recipe~\cite{freedom}, which changes several components
at once, is retained only in the supporting artifact.

Let a training batch contain $B$ triples $(u,i,j)$, with observed item $i$ and
an item $j$ sampled outside that user's training history. With collaborative
embeddings $h$ and score $\hat y_{ui}=h_u^\top h_i$, the averaged BPR loss is
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm BPR}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{(u,i,j)}
 \operatorname{softplus}(\hat y_{uj}-\hat y_{ui}).
\end{equation}
A shared two-layer MLP $g$ maps frozen 1536-dimensional semantic vectors into
the collaborative dimension. Write $\overline{\mathcal L}_u$,
$\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^+}$ and $\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^-}$ for the
batch-averaged cosine InfoNCE losses on users, positive items and negative
items, respectively. Following the released code, each user target is
contrasted against all projected user profiles; the two item losses use their
respective in-batch item targets. Repeated items are retained in the batch.
Then the full and item-only objectives are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\rm full}&=\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm BPR}
 +\alpha(\overline{\mathcal L}_u+\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^+}
 +\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^-})+\beta\|\Theta\|_2^2,\\
 \mathcal L_{\rm item}&=\overline{\mathcal L}_{\rm BPR}
 +\alpha(\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^+}+\overline{\mathcal L}_{i^-})
 +\beta\|\Theta\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Deleting $\overline{\mathcal L}_u$ leaves the other coefficients unchanged.
It reduces total alignment pressure and changes the shared projector's
supervision, so this is not a comparison at matched total regularization.
Both RLMRec variants retain the released sum of layers 0 through 3, dropout
without survivor rescaling and squared parameter regularization. Cached
embeddings are invalidated before evaluation and after checkpoint restoration.


\section{Reproduction Scope and Process}
\paragraph{Why this system.}
RLMRec provides a concrete instance of a widely applicable design question:
how should generated semantic profiles supervise collaborative recommendation?
Its released interactions, semantic vectors and LightGCN implementation allow
us to hold the inputs fixed while separating the user and item alignment
terms. We focus on RLMRec-Con with this backbone. We do not reproduce every
backbone, augmentation variant or original-paper experiment, and do not claim
numerical equality with its published tables. The contribution is a
released-code reproduction with an objective-level extension and a diagnosis
of implementation and stopping choices that affect its interpretation.

\paragraph{Questions and evidence.}
We ask whether removing only user alignment preserves the measured semantic
gain, whether that contrast is stable across three released benchmarks, and
whether an apparent large loss difference survives a longer stopping budget.
A fourth question concerns execution repeatability at a fixed seed. These
questions require different evidence: five paired seeds for main model
contrasts, validation-only runs for the stopping diagnosis, and repeated
executions of one fixed setting for numerical repeatability. We keep those
samples separate rather than pooling them as additional independent seeds.

\paragraph{Reconstructing the implementation.}
The local audit identifies the RLMRec release at commit
\texttt{22413752246de3dee8ab0d509f7f7a8889080f95}. The important reconstruction
choices are the released sum readout, edge dropout without survivor rescaling,
global squared parameter regularization, and the distinct user and item
contrastive candidate sets. Earlier local LightGCN variants used different
readouts or regularization and are excluded from the main comparison.
Agreement on a model name is therefore insufficient to establish that two
experiments use the same objective or propagation operator.

The local implementation invalidates its embedding cache before evaluation
and after restoring a checkpoint. Otherwise evaluation may use embeddings
computed from earlier weights. This is a deliberate implementation correction,
not an additional model component; we do not have a controlled benchmark
estimate of its isolated effect on accuracy. The tests cover the retained
objective, model wiring, graph operators and cache behavior. Passing them
supports these implementation properties rather than establishing equality
with every upstream training outcome.

\paragraph{Changes and acquisition limits.}
We reuse the supplied semantic vectors rather than regenerating profiles.
Training uses Apple MPS, with a gather/scatter implementation of sparse
propagation. Dataset-specific batches and stopping budgets are stated below;
they are changes in experimental setup that limit a strict replication claim.
The evidence package records public-source and local-file audits, but no
upstream-author correspondence. We cannot infer that undocumented details
were confirmed by the original authors. Nor have we byte-compared these local
inputs with a newly downloaded complete release.

\paragraph{What can be reproduced from the package.}
The artifact supports three progressively stronger checks. First, a reader
can verify file fingerprints and recalculate every reported main contrast
from frozen JSON results without raw data or accelerator hardware. Second,
with the released inputs installed, the reader can verify split disjointness,
array shapes and input fingerprints. Third, the training entry point reruns a
selected manifest configuration into a separate output directory. A two-epoch
smoke run exercises the pipeline but is not a reproduction of the scientific
result. Identical seeds do not guarantee identical MPS outcomes.

The main manifest fixes 60 runs: four models, three datasets and five seeds.
This paper reports 45 of them, omitting the graph-recipe comparison from its
main tables while retaining it in the multiplicity family and artifact.
The package also includes all twelve weight-grid cells and twelve diagnostic
executions. Only the weight grid has a fully preserved pre-run source snapshot;
source digests for earlier and later runs do not recover missing source bytes.
This distinction prevents table reproducibility from being mistaken for a
complete reconstruction of every historical execution.

\section{Experimental Design}
\paragraph{Inputs and ranking.}
We reuse RLMRec's released interactions and semantic embeddings. Amazon-book
contains 11,000 users, 9,332 items and 120,464 training, 40,290 validation and
40,106 test interactions. Yelp contains 11,091 users, 11,010 items
and 166,620 training, 55,479 validation and 55,436 test interactions.
Steam contains 23,310 users, 5,237 items and 316,190/104,897/104,835
training/validation/test interactions. We verified that the split matrices
have disjoint nonzero pairs and that the semantic inputs have the expected
dimensions and finite values. Collaborative adjacency uses training data only.
All-ranking evaluation masks training positives; validation positives remain
eligible test candidates. Recall and NDCG are macro-averaged over users with
positives in the evaluated split.

We cannot verify a complete split-filtered generation history for every
released semantic profile. The upstream examples use GPT-3.5-turbo and
text-embedding-ada-002, but examples are not authenticated generation logs for
the full release. Our results are conditional on these fixed semantic inputs;
disjoint interaction splits alone do not certify absence of information
contamination in upstream text generation.

\paragraph{Configurations and selection.}
All runs use Apple MPS. Compared models use 32-dimensional collaborative embeddings, three
collaborative layers, Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and no optimizer
weight decay. Full and item-only RLMRec share $\alpha=0.01$, temperature
$0.2$, keep rate $0.8$ and $\beta=10^{-7}$. Batch sizes are 1024 for Amazon
and 4096 for Yelp and Steam, fixed within each dataset. Consequently, cross-dataset
behavior is not an isolated effect of dataset identity. The semantic models
have 1,880,752 trainable parameters on Amazon and 1,937,360 on Yelp.

We evaluate validation Recall@20 every three epochs and restore its best
checkpoint before a single final test evaluation per run. Patience is five
non-improving evaluations for Amazon and Yelp and 25 for Steam. Steam is a
later extension with a longer stopping budget, not an identical-protocol
replication across datasets. The five paired seeds are 1, 7, 13, 42 and 2024.
All 45 runs in the three-model comparison stopped early rather than hitting
a time or epoch cap. Existing development and test results informed this
retrospective study; its framing is not preregistered.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and sensitivity.}
We report sample standard deviations and paired 95\% Student-$t$ intervals
across the five seeds, with paired $t$-tests and exhaustive two-sided sign-flip
checks. Holm adjustment covers twelve contrasts per metric: full and item-only
versus the baseline, and item-only and the archived graph recipe versus full,
on each of three datasets. Intervals are pointwise, not multiplicity-adjusted.
The family includes the archived comparisons even though they are omitted
from the main tables. Five seeds quantify run variation on fixed inputs,
not uncertainty across populations; no equivalence margin was selected.

The original validation-only grid runs full and item-only RLMRec at
$\alpha\in\{0.003,0.01,0.03\}$ on development seed 2023 for Amazon and Yelp.
All twelve cells are retained. To diagnose the high-weight Yelp result, four
additional runs use $\alpha=0.03$, patience 20 and seeds 2023 and 2025, with
other settings unchanged. They evaluate no test metrics. This follow-up is
motivated by the observed failure and is not a new unbiased test comparison.

Separately, each RLMRec variant was executed four times at the fixed Amazon
setting $\alpha=0.01$, seed 42 and patience five. Each repeat reports its
validation-selected test score. These eight executions measure repeatability;
they are not added to the five-seed sample or used to select a configuration.

\paragraph{Marginal contribution of user alignment.}
Full and item-only RLMRec improve on the collaborative baseline on every
dataset (Table~\ref{tab:main}); all six paired $t$ comparisons survive Holm
adjustment, with adjusted $p\leq0.020$. The item-only gains in Recall@20 are
$0.00760$, $0.00959$ and $0.00907$ on Amazon, Yelp and Steam. These gains show
that a semantic objective without the user term can be effective in this
implementation. They do not assign all of full RLMRec's benefit to item
information: the objectives share a projector and differ in total loss weight.

For Amazon and Yelp, Table~\ref{tab:effects} reports changes of $-0.000266$ and $-0.000151$. Their pointwise
intervals span zero but admit losses as large as approximately 1.25\% and
2.08\% of full RLMRec's mean; neither establishes practical equivalence.

pointwise interval excluding zero and Holm-adjusted paired $t$ value
$p=0.0419$. Thus the data include a setting where user alignment helps.
With only five pairs, the exact sign-flip test cannot attain a two-sided
value below $0.0625$; the Steam inference depends on the parametric test.
The inference is restricted to the tested Steam protocol.

\subsection{Diagnosing early stopping}
\label{sec:stopping}
Under patience 20, full RLMRec at Yelp $\alpha=0.03$ reaches validation
Recall@20 of $0.11587$ and $0.11605$ on seeds 2023 and 2025. Item-only reaches
$0.11866$ and $0.11991$ (Table~\ref{tab:patience}). The original seed-2023
short-patience gap was $0.08085$; the long-patience follow-up gap is $0.00280$.
Full RLMRec still trails, but the severe apparent collapse does not persist.

The trajectories explain why (Figure~\ref{fig:patience}). Both full-model
runs initially fall below their epoch-0 validation values. Replaying the
five-check stopping rule on their logged validation values would stop both
at epoch index 15, before recovery; with more patience, their best epochs
are 84 and 72. The logs round Recall@20 to four decimals, so replay is an
approximation, although these early deficits are much larger than rounding.
This within-trajectory check avoids assuming that the original and repeated
MPS runs are bitwise identical. It supports premature stopping as the cause
of the severe low-score outcome, without identifying its optimization cause.

\subsection{Fixed-seed repeatability}
\label{sec:resolution}
The four Amazon repeats have test Recall@20 SD $0.000536$ for full RLMRec
and $0.000344$ for item-only, with ranges $0.001090$ and $0.000810$.
Full-model selected epoch indices are 66, 78, 66 and 72; item-only selects
66 in all four executions. Execution variation is therefore present even
when checkpoint epoch is unchanged. Both SDs exceed the absolute Amazon
five-seed mean contrast of $0.000266$.

This observation cautions against interpreting the small Amazon point
estimate precisely. It does not invalidate pairing, establish a universal
detection threshold, or prove that the observed variation is caused solely
by MPS scatter operations. Four repeats at one seed cannot decompose
execution and initialization variance or be transferred to Yelp and Steam.
The primary uncertainty estimates remain the paired seed intervals.

\section{Implications and Limitations}
The combined evidence supports a specific practical conclusion: evaluate the
user term separately and inspect stopping trajectories before interpreting a
large ablation gap. Semantic augmentation helps without user alignment in
all three tested settings, but Steam shows a measurable incremental user-term
benefit under the paired $t$ analysis. The high-weight Yelp result demonstrates
how stopping can greatly exaggerate that benefit's apparent reversal.

Our scope is one backbone, three fixed benchmarks and released semantic
inputs with incompletely verified generation provenance. Dataset-specific
batch sizes and patience prevent attributing all differences to dataset
identity. The diagnostics neither optimize model performance nor establish
equivalence. Removing a loss also changes shared-projector supervision and
total alignment pressure.

Historical results and logs carry source and data digests, but complete
source bytes are not recovered for every run, including the follow-up
diagnostics. The original weight grid has a preserved pre-run snapshot.
The artifact distinguishes these evidence levels and retains all scores,
checksummed manifests, current code and table-generation scripts.
Removing user alignment still leaves collaborative user histories and
potentially user-authored item reviews in use. We establish no new-user
cold-start, privacy or profile-generation-cost benefit.

result manifests, archived controls and analysis scripts. It also contains
